How to build your car
  1. Name your car, then add body shapes from Your Parts Studio.
  2. Drag a part to move it. Use the blue handle to change its length, the gold handle to change its height, or the corner for both.
  3. Hold Shift while resizing to keep proportions. Otherwise dimensions change independently.
  4. Add windows, a windshield, wheels, lights, and axles. Every part can move, rotate, stretch, and change color.
  5. Choose Photo windshield to insert a picture. Its outline and proportions are editable.
  6. Parts cannot pass through the ground line, even when moved, resized, or rotated. Place tires using “Ground wheel” and leave space below the chassis for air.
  7. Each grid square represents 6 inches (½ foot). Two squares equal 1 foot. Measurements and Snap both use this same scale.
  8. Open Wind Tunnel. It starts at 0 mph; move the slider to start the air.
  9. The default between-wheel section shows a slice between the left and right tires. Ghosted tires are outside that slice; body and axle parts still block air.
  10. Underbody emphasis adds brighter tracers to open gaps. It changes visibility, not the amount of simulated air.
  11. Try changes and record results in your notebook. IRL Statistics contains classroom estimates in U.S. units.

Shortcuts: Delete removes a selected part. Ctrl/⌘ + D duplicates. Ctrl/⌘ + Z undoes.

How the models work

The tunnel uses a two-dimensional lattice-Boltzmann fluid solver with bounce-back solid boundaries. The default center section excludes outboard tires and includes the body and any added axles. Switch it off to test a solid wheel side silhouette. Extra tracers only highlight the existing velocity field; they do not force air into blocked gaps.

These are educational, normalized flow simulations, not validated three-dimensional automotive CFD. MPG, drag, and power are comparative estimates using a silhouette-based drag coefficient, an estimated frontal area, rolling resistance, road grade, and engine efficiency. Airflow around wheels in three dimensions, rotating wheels, and detailed suspension are not modeled. Lengths are shown in inches or feet, speed in mph, force in lbf, power in horsepower, and fuel in U.S. gallons.
